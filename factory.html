HOW TO READ THE FACTORY

Faster at one station.
More phones at the end?

The important number is phones leaving packaging. Each phone consumes one kit and passes through all five stations. Small stacks show waiting parts; supply and output boxes are summarized visually. Conveyor movement forms part of each station's cycle.

You start with ten people. Each robot covers one human task and arrives from outside. No new robots are built here: this scene shows the effect of automating a line.

Humans
Two per station. One shift: 08–12 and 14–18.
Robots
Up to two per station. Each has 21 h available, 2 h charging and 1 h maintenance, with staggered schedules.
Rate
A robot contributes 1.4 times a person's rate in this example. Machinery also limits output.
Materials
160 initial kits and up to 160 new kits daily at 08:00. They include a circuit board and RAM, sensors, housing, screen and battery. Up to 16 parts can wait between stations.
Comparison
The trial holds the setup fixed for a full 24 hours. It includes waiting and breaks; it does not simply multiply one hour's rate by 24.

All values are learning assumptions, not real productivity figures or predicted timelines. A human factory can organize multiple shifts. Breakdowns, rejected units, costs and real station adaptation time are omitted.
