This laboratory compares a brain with a language model, but their parts are not equivalent. The brain shape is an illustration: the dots compress enormous quantities and do not simulate firing neurons. A census of adult male brains estimated an average of roughly eighty-six billion neurons and eighty-five billion non-neuronal cells. These are sample estimates, not identical counts for every person. The cerebellum contains a large share of the neurons despite occupying less volume; counting cells does not measure intelligence. Beside it we use the published Grok one architecture from March twenty twenty-four: three hundred and fourteen billion parameters, sixty-four layers and eight experts, with two selected per token. A parameter is a learned number, not a cell. The boxes are a compressed diagram. They describe neither current Grok versions nor an equivalence between a brain and a computer.
