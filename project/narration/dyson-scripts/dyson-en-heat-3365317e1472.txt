Orange markers show infrared in false color. A plate at about three hundred and thirty-one kelvin does not look red hot. That figure describes an isolated black plate at one astronomical unit, with two faces radiating freely. It is not a calculated temperature for a dense swarm: other collectors could block or return radiation. The graph compares reference blackbody spectra. A colder emitter peaks at longer wavelengths. An infrared excess would be a clue to investigate, not proof of technology: dust and other natural processes can also produce it. Change the radius or emissivity and follow the shifting peak.
